DECK #

The dovetail keys stop a box sliding sideways, but any box lifts straight out. The deck is a printed base the boxes snap down onto, so a whole layout picks up and travels as one piece.

a base, not a box · no gears, no lid, no cells, no cost · bone · tiles 1×1, 2×2 and 3×3 exported

In the standard

DECK is part of the box standard ("The deck"): every shell now has a socket under each grid node, cut by gbs_shell_geom(), so every box gets it with no change to its own description. Paul chose the deck over the other two locks (loose clips from below, twist keys on top), and it went into the standard without a test print first. The fit check has a new test (13 in tools/check_fit.sh): every box sits on a deck of its own size and touches no peg. Deck tiles are exported at 1×1, 2×2 and 3×3. Nothing is printed yet. Old shells still sit beside new ones; they just don't snap to a deck until they are reprinted.

Boxes on a deck

Why the lock sits at the grid nodes

Every box, whatever its size, has its corners on the 64 mm grid. A grid node, the corner of a cell, is where up to four boxes meet. A 1×2 box has two more nodes along its long sides, a 2×2 has one in the middle, and the 3×3 ORRERY has four inside. So the lock lives at the nodes, and one rule covers every box:

  • Every node under a box gets a socket in the bottom of the plinth: a quarter at the box's corners, a half along its sides, a whole one inside a big box. The box's own footprint trims one round socket to the right piece, so nothing in the box description changes.
  • The deck has a split peg at every node. Its cross slot runs along the grid lines, so its four fingers sit in the four quadrants. Each finger is under one box and hooks that box alone. A 1×1 box gets one finger at each corner; a 1×2 gets six.

How it works

You press a box down onto the deck the same way you lower it beside its neighbors today. The fingers spring in through the socket's bore, and the barbs spring out above a ledge 2.7 mm up.

Pull firmly and the 45 degree backs of the barbs push the fingers in again, so boxes come off for a new layout. Nothing changes on the sides: a box with sockets still stands flat on a table.

Peg6.0 mm across, four fingers 1.2 mm thick (three 0.4 mm perimeters), 1.0 mm slots on the grid lines, 3.8 mm tall above the deck
Barb0.45 mm proud, its top at 3.3 mm, 45 degrees under and over
Socketbore 6.2 mm (0.1 clear), ledge at 2.7 mm, seat 7.6 mm to 4.0 mm, 0.4 mm chamfer at the mouth; 1.0 mm of plinth over it
Springthe fingers stand in a pit 3.2 mm into the deck, so each is about 7 mm long; strain on the way in is about 1.5 %, inside PLA's limit for a snap used again and again
Decka rib 10 mm wide and 4 mm tall on every grid line (5 mm under each plinth), a 13 mm pad at each node, open windows in the cells

Tiles

A tile is any size up to 3×3 (192 mm, which fits the P1S plate with room). Its edge ribs are half width and its edge pegs keep only the fingers over the tile, so two tiles side by side make whole ribs and whole pegs. Tiles have no joint of their own: boxes dovetailed across a seam tie the two tiles together. A 3×3 tile is about 45 cm³ solid, much less at 15 % infill.

Big boxes

A node inside a box (2×2 and up) needs a boss on the floor to hold its socket: 10 mm across, from the floor to the plinth top (z 2 to 5), under the first gear layer. tools/lock_nodes.scad lists what stands near each inner node:

  • ORRERY: nothing within 25 mm of its four inner nodes. Clear.
  • BRAIN: the motor driver's tray ends 4.9 mm from the node; the boss merges with its solid rim and misses the pocket. Clear.
  • DRIVE: the battery pack's east edge was 4.5 mm from the node, inside the boss. The pack moved 1 mm west, to [-32, 3], which clears it.
  • SENSE: the pack's southwest corner was 3.5 mm from the node. The pack moved 2 mm east, to [32, 14].

To test first

  1. Print a 1×1 deck (cells = [1, 1]) and one WIRE shell with sockets.
  2. Check that the box clicks down, that it lifts the deck with it, and that a firm pull takes it off.
  3. If the snap is too stiff, drop lk_barb to 0.35; too weak, raise it to 0.55. PETG flexes better than PLA if the fingers crack.

What it teaches

  • Snap fits: a flexing finger and a barb hold by shape, not glue, and the angle on the barb's back decides how hard it is to pull off.
  • Strain limits: a long finger bends further for the same strain, which is why the pegs stand in a pit in the deck.
  • One rule for every size: putting the lock on the grid nodes, which every box shares, means no box needs its own design.
  • Tiling: half features at the edges add up to whole ones when two tiles meet.
