Torque cost

3 of the crank's 20 stages · 3 of the motor's 15

3 stages to E1, 5 stages to E2 and to N. Count the N side as 5 when you add up a column. Two taps would cost 3 each, but a tap ends the column; the bus keeps it going.

How it works

The lower cell is the TAP box. The idler I1 adds the mesh that keeps E1 forward, and C1, with a 24-tooth gear on each layer, drops the line from the N/S layer (2) to the E/W layer (1) for E1.

C1's layer-2 gear also drives J, a 40-tooth idler in the middle of the box. J's only job is to carry the line 64 mm north, one cell, to C2. Its dial shows the line.

C2 is the TEE's stack: one 24-tooth gear on each layer, so it drives E2 on layer 1 and the north port on layer 2 at once.

Idlers never change the ratio and every stack is 24:24, so all three outputs run at ×1. An odd number of meshes on every path keeps them forward.

Why it is built this way

  • On the power line. The old BUS put three outputs on one side (lanes A, B, C), but nothing takes three lanes in. Rows and columns are what boxes actually join on.
  • Two cells tall, not wide. A column box sits in the power column; each of its cells starts a row, so one drive feeds two rows.
  • N passes on. The tap ends the column; the bus doesn't, so a second BUS or a TAP can sit above it.
  • Reused stacks. The lower cell is the tap's train and the upper cell is the tee's, both fit-checked, joined by one idler. J is 40 teeth because C1 and C2 are exactly 64 mm apart: 12 + 20 + 20 + 12.

How to assemble it

  1. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin. The pin is the bigger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it.
  2. Build it in this order: the four ports, then I1, C1 and C2, then J, then the keepers and lid, then the dial.
  3. Put a keeper on each side that has ports: its legs go in the notches and its flange in the recess on the wall top.
  4. Put the lid on. Its corner holes take the pins on the corner blocks.

To join it to a neighbor, lower it onto the neighbor's tongues from above.

What it teaches

  • Power distribution: one source, several branches, like a bus bar feeding branch circuits.
  • Idlers carry motion across a distance without changing the ratio.
  • Cost adds along a path: the far outputs pay for the extra two meshes.
