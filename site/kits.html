Kit 1 · the starter kit

First Turns

Nine boxes and one crank. Count turns, make the line faster, slower and backwards, split it, and carry it up a row. Every other kit adds to this one.

CRANK×1TURNS×1×2×1÷4×1REVERSE×1TEE×1WIRE×1TURN×1TAP×1

What it teaches

  • A drive, and what "forward" means: the crank turns its outputs counter-clockwise.
  • Gear ratios multiply: ×2 then ÷4 is ×½, in either order.
  • Every mesh flips the direction, so an idler (REVERSE) turns the line backwards.
  • One line can feed two (TEE), and two lines can cross in one box without touching (WIRE).
  • The N/S power line: TURN sends a line north, TAP brings it back east one row up.

Filament: 476 g, 84 parts

Shells and keepers 275 g bone · lids Drives 11 g, Readouts 11 g, Ratios 33 g, Routing 44 g · gears and parts 101 g in the role colors.

Bought

Nothing. A PTFE dry lube for the pins (every kit uses it).

Builds

1Count your turns

Turn the crank once and TURNS reads 1.0. Half a turn reads 0.5. This is the measuring stick for every build after it.

longest path 4 of the crank's 20 stages

2Faster and slower

Four crank turns read 2.0: ×2 then ÷4 is ×½. Swap ×2 and ÷4 round and the answer is the same, because ratios multiply. Watch ÷4's dial crawl while the crank runs.

longest path 10 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

3Backwards

REVERSE puts one more gear in the line, so TURNS counts down: 9.9, 9.8, 9.7. Count the gears from the crank to the reading: an odd count keeps forward, an even count flips it.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages

4Up a row

TURN takes the line north; TAP turns it east again one row up. Together they do what RISER does in one tall box.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

5The whole kit

The crank feeds east and north at once. TEE splits the east line, and its north branch crosses the TAP's line inside WIRE: two lines through one box, each on its own channel. Every box turns.

Kit 2 · the clockwork kit

Tick Tock

Store energy, spend it slowly, and keep time. A wind-up spring motor, an escapement, a speed governor, a flywheel, a ratchet and a clock face with three hands: the parts inside every clock.

SPRING×1TICK×1TOLL×1GOVERNOR×1FLYWHEEL×1RATCHET×1FACE×1

Also uses from First Turns: CRANK, TURNS.

What it teaches

  • Energy can be stored (a wound spring, a spinning disc) and given back.
  • A ratchet lets motion through one way only, the way a winding key works.
  • Inertia: weight at the rim keeps a wheel turning after you let go.
  • An escapement turns a steady push into equal ticks. That is what makes a clock a clock.
  • A governor brakes only when the line runs fast: feedback without a single electric part.
  • A gear train counts: FACE divides TICK's beats by 60, 60 and 12, like any clock. Its time is only as good as the beat.

Filament: 523 g, 76 parts

Shells and keepers 270 g bone · lids Drives 11 g, Motion shapers 46 g, Readouts 58 g · gears and parts 139 g in the role colors.

Bought

Ten 6 mm steel balls (six for FLYWHEEL, two each for TICK and GOVERNOR) and a 4 mm steel rod, 32 mm long, for FACE's hands. The mainspring prints in PETG. A strip of felt for GOVERNOR is optional.

Builds

1One way only

Turn the crank forward and TURNS counts. Turn it back and the pawl stops it. Push the ratchet's tab and it lets go.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages

2Keep it spinning

Spin the crank hard and let go: count how far the line coasts on TURNS. Press the steel balls into FLYWHEEL's rim and try again. Weight at the rim counts most.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages

3Wind it up

Wind SPRING's red knob a turn and lift the plunger. GOVERNOR's shoes fly out and hold the line at a steady pace instead of letting it whiz round in a second. Swap GOVERNOR for FLYWHEEL and compare.

longest path 3 of SPRING's 6 stages

4A clock

Wind SPRING and lift the plunger. TICK lets the line through one tooth per swing of its foliot, and TOLL counts the turns as they go past. Move TICK's balls out and the clock runs slower.

longest path 6 of SPRING's 6 stages

5A clock with a face

TEE splits SPRING's line: the east branch runs TICK, which sets the pace for everything, and the north branch turns FACE. One beat is one second on the seconds hand. A full wind runs it about 37 seconds. Push the minute hand round to set the time.

longest path 6 of SPRING's 6 stages

  • A clock is SPRING's whole budget. SPRING drives one or two boxes (its doc). Builds 4 and 5 each cost 6 stages, the sandbox's guess at SPRING's limit. FACE counts 8 stages by its meshes but loads the line like a 3 or 4, because everything after its first stack turns 60 to 720 times slower (its doc); the sandbox counts it as 3. Time a full wind on the first print.
Kit 3 · the switches and clocks kit

Gear Logic

Make the gears choose. Switch a line by hand, pick between two sources, let a clock signal do the switching, flip on every turn, and remember that something happened.

ROUTE×1PICK×1STEER×1SQUARE×1SWAP×1TRIP×1TAP×1TURNS×1

Also uses from First Turns: CRANK, TURNS, TAP, TURN, WIRE, ÷4, TEE.

What it teaches

  • if / else: ROUTE sends the line one way or the other.
  • A multiplexer: PICK chooses which of two lines drives the output.
  • A clock signal is a level, high or low, that switches another box (SQUARE into STEER).
  • Duty cycle: turn SQUARE's cam plate a click and the high part gets longer.
  • A T flip-flop: SWAP's output changes every turn, E, N, E, N.
  • One bit of memory: TRIP's flag stays up until you reset it.

Filament: 484 g, 118 parts

Shells and keepers 269 g bone · lids Switches and clocks 68 g, Motion shapers 14 g, Routing 11 g, Readouts 11 g · gears and parts 112 g in the role colors.

Bought

A rubber band for SQUARE (size in its doc) and a pen spring for TRIP.

Builds

1Choose a way

Turn ROUTE's knob to E and the first TURNS counts; turn it to N and the line goes up through TAP to the second. Between the two rests nothing turns: break before make.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

2Choose a source

Two lines reach PICK: one at full speed through TAP and WIRE, one at a quarter speed through ÷4 and TURN. The knob picks which one drives TURNS. A gear selector made of a switch.

longest path 13 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

3A clock decides

SQUARE turns the crank's line into a level: high, then low, once a cycle. STEER reads it and sends the main line to E while it's high and to N while it's low. Watch the two counters take turns, then change the duty and watch the split change.

longest path 11 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

4Every other turn

SWAP sends the line out of E for one turn and N for the next. The two counters end up a turn apart: a divide-by-two, the first stage of a binary counter.

longest path 12 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

5Remember it happened

TEE feeds TRIP and a TURNS side by side. TRIP's flag pops up when its cam comes round and stays up while the counter runs on. Push the reset tab to clear it: one bit of memory.

longest path 7 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

  • Two TAPs and two TURNS appear in builds 1, 3 and 4: one of each from the Starter, one of each in this kit.
Kit 4 · the arithmetic kit

Gear Calculator

Do sums with gears. Add two motions, subtract one from another, scale by a constant, and count past 99. This is how the differential analyzers of the 1930s computed.

ADDER×1CRANK×1÷2×1TALLY×1TURNS×1

Also uses from First Turns: ×2, REVERSE, ÷4, TURNS.

What it teaches

  • A differential adds: ADDER's output is the average of its two inputs, (W + S) / 2.
  • Constants are gear ratios: ×2 after ADDER turns the average into the sum.
  • Subtraction is addition of a reversed line.
  • Inverses: ×2 then ÷2 gives back what you put in.
  • Place value: TALLY's carries do with gears what you do with pencil and paper.

Filament: 364 g, 68 parts

Shells and keepers 198 g bone · lids Ratios 34 g, Drives 11 g, Readouts 33 g · gears and parts 88 g in the role colors.

Bought

Nothing.

Builds

1Add two motions

Two cranks drive ADDER. The lower crank's own TURNS shows S. Turn the left crank 3 times and the lower one 2 times, in any order: the top TURNS reads 5.0. ADDER gives (W + S) / 2 and ×2 doubles it back.

longest path 12 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

2Subtract

Put REVERSE in front of W and the same build gives S − W. Turn the lower crank 5 times and the left one 2 times: it reads 3.0.

longest path 15 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

3Undo a ratio

×2 then ÷2: one crank turn reads 1.0 again. Swap ÷2 for the Starter's ÷4 and predict the reading before you turn.

longest path 10 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

4Count big

×2 into TALLY: every crank turn adds 2.0. How many turns to reach 50.0? Watch the carry ripple when the tenths roll over.

longest path 11 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

  • Turn the cranks one at a time, or hold one still, while you learn it. Turning both at once works too: that is the point of a differential.
Kit 5 · the machines kit

Motion and Power

A 9 V motor, a gearbox you shift by hand, and the classic mechanisms: a Geneva step, a cam, a Scotch yoke, a worm that lifts a bucket and holds it, and a plotter that draws what you do.

MOTOR×1CLUTCH×1SHIFT×1GENEVA×1CAM×1YOKE×1HOIST×1PLOT×1

Also uses from First Turns: TURNS, TEE, TAP, CRANK.

What it teaches

  • A motor is a drive with a budget: it turns fewer boxes than a hand.
  • A slip clutch protects a line and lets you change gear under way.
  • A transmission trades speed for torque: 1st gear is half speed, twice the push.
  • Intermittent motion: a Geneva wheel steps and locks.
  • Rotation to straight-line motion: a cam lifts, a Scotch yoke slides in a sine.
  • A worm gives a big ratio in one mesh and holds its load: the wheel can't turn the worm back.
  • Time on one axis, a signal on the other: an oscilloscope.

Filament: 557 g, 102 parts

Shells and keepers 291 g bone · lids Drives 23 g, Motion shapers 56 g, Ratios 11 g, Readouts 25 g · gears and parts 151 g in the role colors.

Bought

MOTOR's parts: an N20 12 V 150 rpm gearmotor, a 9 V (PP3) battery and snap, a DPDT ON-OFF-ON mini toggle, 26 AWG wire and heat-shrink (bill of materials in MOTOR's doc). A TPU pad, or felt or a rubber washer cut to 22 mm, for CLUTCH. 14 mm of 1.75 mm filament for PLOT's last tread pin. For HOIST, a 4 mm steel rod 21.9 mm long and about 150 mm of 0.3 to 0.5 mm braided line.

Builds

1Change gear

MOTOR runs CLUTCH, SHIFT and TURNS. Move SHIFT's stick from 2nd to 1st and the count slows to half. With CLUTCH in front, a gear change under way slips instead of grinding.

longest path 12 of the motor's 15 stages · readings want a ±

2Steps and lifts

TEE splits the motor's line. GENEVA's dial steps a quarter turn and locks each time round; CAM's follower rod rises and falls through the lid. Swap CAM's sine plate for the snail or the heart.

longest path 6 of the motor's 15 stages · readings want a ±

3Round to straight

YOKE turns MOTOR's turning into a green tab sliding 20 mm side to side, a sine against time.

longest path 2 of the motor's 15 stages

4Draw with your hand

MOTOR drives PLOT's W port and sweeps the spot across the lid at a steady pace: that's time. The crank under PLOT's west cell drives S and moves the whole tread north and south: that's your signal. Wiggle it and watch your hand drawn against time; turn it steadily and you draw a sine wave.

longest path 7 of the smaller drive's 15 stages

5Lift and hold

MOTOR winds HOIST's bucket up the mast through CLUTCH, about 2 mm a port turn. Switch the motor off and the bucket stays put: the worm holds it with no pawl and no brake. At the top, CLUTCH slips instead of snapping the string. Reverse the motor to let the bucket down.

longest path 12 of the motor's 15 stages

  • PLOT takes time on W and the signal on S, from the box under its west cell, so the crank sits right under it and build 4 needs no routing at all.
Kit 6 · the chance and sound kit

Party

Six boxes for games and music. Spin a wheel of fortune, let the line pick its own way, deal a number from gears and string, then play a drummer, a boom and a programmable drum machine.

SPIN×1FORK×1SHUFFLE×1BEAT×1BOOM×1ROLL×1

Also uses from First Turns: CRANK, TURNS, TAP, TEE.

What it teaches

  • Gears can't be random, but your hand is: SPIN turns a tiny difference in how hard you crank into a different number.
  • A random "if": FORK sends the line out of E or N, and nobody chose which.
  • Pseudo-random: SHUFFLE looks random but repeats after 30,381 turns, like rand() in a program.
  • Rhythm and frequency: crank faster and BEAT taps faster.
  • Energy stored slowly, let go at once: BOOM winds a band for a whole turn, then fires.
  • Programs as data: ROLL's peg disc is a program, and you swap it to play another one.

Filament: 470 g, 93 parts

Shells and keepers 242 g bone · lids Chance 48 g, Sound 55 g · gears and parts 124 g in the role colors.

Bought

6 mm steel balls: six for SPIN, one each for BEAT and BOOM, three for ROLL, and six more for FORK if you want them. For ROLL, one kalimba tine and two M3 × 8 screws. A felt strip and dots, packing tape (or a balloon for BOOM's drum skin) and a loom band for BOOM. For SHUFFLE, about 40 cm of 0.3 mm braided line and a small rubber band. Offcuts of 1.75 mm filament for axles and pins.

Builds

1Spin the wheel

Crank hard and let go. SPIN coasts, clicks and parks on a number from 1 to 6. TURNS shows how far you cranked: try to land the same number twice.

longest path 9 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

2Let the line decide

Flick FORK's wheel and lock it: the line leaves on E to BEAT, or on N through TAP to BOOM. Flick again for the next choice.

longest path 8 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

3Deal a number

One crank turn, and SHUFFLE's dial shows a number from 1 to 6. It looks random, but start again from the same place and you get the same numbers: that's a seed.

longest path 4 of the crank's 20 stages

4A drummer

BEAT taps its block in a rhythm and passes the line on to ROLL, which plays eight steps a bar on three voices. Swap ROLL's peg disc for a new program.

longest path 5 of the crank's 20 stages

5The band

TEE splits the crank's line. BEAT and ROLL play the east row, and the north branch runs through TAP to BOOM: click, click, click, BOOM, once a turn.

longest path 8 of the crank's 20 stages · readings want a ±

  • FORK, BEAT and TEE each send the line two ways, so each build here needs only the Starter's one TAP.

How these were checked

  • Each layout ran through analyze() in sim/kin.js, the sandbox's checker: crank 20 stages, motor 15, SPRING 6 (a guess until it is timed).
  • Most counters sit more than 8 meshes from their drive, so STANDARD.md asks for a ± mark: backlash shows only when you turn back.
  • Filament is the solid volume of every printed part at 1.24 g/cm³, measured on the sandbox's meshes of the real parts; the slicer will say a little less.
  • Every box in these kits is fit-checked. The two-cell BUS and METER, BRANCH and the long wires are fit-checked too, but the builds here are small enough for TEE and TAP.
