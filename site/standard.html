The Gear Box standard

Every box follows these rules, so any box sits beside any other and their gears mesh. The numbers live in lib/gbs.scad. A box made to these rules needs no knowledge of its neighbors.

The grid

  • Boxes stand on a 64 mm grid. A box is 1x1 cells (64 x 64 mm), or any n x m cells for bigger mechanisms. Four cells fit across a P1S plate.
  • Boxes never turn. +X is East and +Y is North everywhere.
  • West and South are inputs. East and North are outputs. Motion flows west to east and south to north.
  • Only the plinth (a 5 mm high ring round the bottom) touches a neighbor. The walls above it stand 1.3 mm back from the cell edge, which leaves room for a neighbor's gear teeth. Its underside has a socket at every grid node (see "The deck").

Keys: output always meets input

Dovetail keys on the plinths lock neighbors together. You lower a box onto its neighbors from above; once it's down, it can't be pulled sideways.

  • Tongues stick out of the East and North plinth faces. Sockets are cut into the West and South faces.
  • E/W keys sit 12 mm north of each cell center. N/S keys sit 12 mm east of it.
  • A box that is turned a quarter, half or three-quarter turn can't sit down beside another. Either two tongues meet, or a tongue meets solid plinth. tools/check_fit.sh checks this for every box.
  • Key: 8 mm wide at the face, 3 mm deep, 5 mm tall, slope 1:6, with 0.15 mm of clearance.

The deck: lock a layout as a unit

The keys stop sideways motion, never lifting. To carry a layout, boxes snap down onto a deck.

  • Every grid node under a box has a socket in the bottom of the plinth: a quarter at the box's corners, a half along its sides, a whole one inside a box two or more cells each way. Socket: bore 6.2 mm to a ledge 2.7 mm up, seat 7.6 mm to 4.0 mm.
  • Inside a big box, keep a 10 mm circle round each inner node clear from the floor to z 5: a boss there holds the socket.
  • The deck has a split peg at every node, its slots on the grid lines, so each of its four fingers hooks the one box over it.
  • Deck tiles are any size up to 3x3 and butt together; edge pegs keep only their own fingers, and the boxes tie the tiles.
  • gbs_shell_geom() cuts the sockets and adds the bosses for every box; lib/lock.scad draws the deck, and tools/check_fit.sh checks every box on a deck of its own size. Why these numbers: docs/boxes/deck.md.

Ports: the standard toothing

A port is a gear that pokes 1 mm out through a notch in a wall. When two boxes sit together, the ports meet and mesh.

  • Every gear in the system is module 1, 20 degree pressure angle, no profile shift, 4 mm face.
  • A port gear's pitch circle touches the cell edge. Two ports that meet at the same place on an edge are always exactly one pitch-radius sum apart, so any tooth count meshes with any other. A box can take its input on a 16-tooth gear from a neighbor's 32-tooth gear. Neither box needs to know what the other one uses.
  • Lanes: a port sits on one of three lanes along its side. The lanes are at -16, 0 and +16 mm from the cell center and are named A, B and C (A is the west or south end). Two ports only meet on the same lane.
  • Each lane is its own channel. A side with three ports carries three separate motions, like a three-wire cable. A box could copy one motion onto several lanes, or read them separately; no box in the catalog does yet. A box two cells long has six lanes on that side (three per cell), and its keys repeat per cell.
  • Heights key the kinds: E/W ports run on layer 1 (z 6 to 10). N/S ports run on layer 2 (z 10.4 to 14.4). An E/W gear can never mesh with a N/S gear.
  • How many fit on one side of a cell:
  • Three small: lanes A, B and C. Any two neighboring lanes may have at most 26 teeth between them (for example 13, 13, 13 or 12, 14, 12).
  • Two small: lanes A and C, up to 14 teeth each. The corner blocks set this limit.
  • One large: lane B alone, from 12 up to about 56 teeth.
  • Two large gears on one side need a side two cells long.
  • Inside the box it is the same story. Three separate lanes straight through (W lane A to E lane A, and so on) do not fit one cell. Each lane needs at least two gears between its ports, and the three chains collide. A box like that needs to be two cells long.
  • Use at least 12 teeth. Fewer teeth undercut without profile shift, and profile shift is not allowed because it would move the pitch circle off the edge.

What a port carries

  • What crosses an edge is pitch-line motion, measured in millimeters of pitch circle. A mesh passes it on unchanged, whatever the two tooth counts. Only a stack inside a box (two gears of different sizes on one shaft) changes it.
  • Forward means an output port gear turns counter-clockwise seen from above. The input gear it drives then turns clockwise.
  • Every mesh reverses rotation. A box that keeps the direction has an odd number of meshes between its input and its output. The wire box has one, the tee has three, and the reverse box has two.
  • A box's job is written as a ratio of pitch-line speeds, for example x1 (wire, tee), x-1 (reverse) or x1/4 (quarter).
  • E/W is the main line. N/S is a second channel. The crank drives both forward at x1.
  • N/S is also the power line. Every drive box feeds N at x1 as well as E. A column of wire boxes carries that north, and a tap box (S in, E out, the turn box mirrored) restarts a row with fresh torque at x1. A box that needs force (a carry, a Geneva, a clutch) sits behind a tap, not five boxes down a row. A tap repeats only the x1 signal; a box that needs a different signal re-driven needs a second drive box.

Torque budget

One drive reaches only so far, and the limit is drag, not strength. Count in stages: one stage is one mesh plus one pin. A dry printed stage on a 16-tooth gear passes about 0.86 of its torque (teeth about 0.93, the pin about 0.92), and every stack adds a small fixed floor.

  • Every box declares a cost in stages along its path: wire 2, tee 3, turn 3, tap 3, reverse 3, quarter 3, bus 3 (5 to its upper outputs), meter 2 (5 on the line), branch 3 (3 on north), long wires 2 a cell, riser 4, counter 4, Geneva 3, ratchet 3, clutch 3, adder 5. The cost goes in the catalog comment and in the README table.
  • Every drive declares a budget: crank 20, motor 15. Set these from the first prints (hang a weight from the crank knob and find the lightest that keeps the chain turning) and correct this list.
  • Rule: along any path from a drive, the summed cost of the boxes may not exceed the drive's budget. Taps and extra drives start a new path.
  • Readings: backlash is about 0.2 mm per mesh at the pitch line, 1.4 degrees on a 16-tooth gear, and it shows on reversal. A dial or counter sits within 8 meshes of its drive, or its lid carries a "±" mark.
  • Keep module 1. The teeth carry twenty times these loads; a bigger module would cost lanes and buy nothing.

Inside a box

  • Layers: four gear layers. Their bottoms are at z = 6, 10.4, 14.8 and 19.2, each 4 mm thick with 0.4 mm gaps between them. The lid's underside is at 23.6, the lid is 2.4 mm thick, and the top is at 26.
  • Stacks: each gear, or set of gears that turn together, is a stack. A stack turns on a fixed 4 mm pin printed with the shell, with a 4.4 mm bore. It stands on an 8 mm column with 0.2 mm of play above and below. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin before its stack goes on: the pin is the larger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it. Every center distance is an exact pitch-radius sum: place stacks with gbs_meet(), never by eye.
  • Holding stacks down:
  • Port gears are held by keepers, one per side. A keeper is a T-shaped piece: its legs fill the notches above the side's port gears and its flange sits in a recess in the wall top. The lid holds it.
  • An inner stack holds itself with a hub up to the lid.
  • A stack can also sit under another stack's bigger gear, which holds it (none). It must go in before that stack.
  • A stack printed upside down is held by the lid itself (when its top gear is on layer 4) or by a loose sleeve on its pin.
  • A dial or crank stack has its hub stop just under the lid. The shoulder there holds the stack down, and a thinner shaft comes up through the lid. The dial or crank wheel presses onto the D-key at its top.
  • Digit wheels: a wheel stack ends in a D-key just under the lid, and a digit wheel (a disc with inlaid digits on its top face) presses onto it. The lid has a window over each wheel. Digits come upright as they reach their window. A tall stack (the carry pinion) reaches up to just under the wheels, which hold it down.
  • Odometer carry: the pocket tools' tread-counter carry, laid flat (gbs_odo2d() in lib/gbs.scad). The lower digit's stack carries a locking ring with one notch and one carry tooth. A 10-tooth pinion has short and long teeth on that layer and full teeth on the next, where it drives the upper digit's 20-tooth gear. Between carries the pinion is locked. As the lower digit goes from 9 to 0 it kicks the pinion 72 degrees, which moves the upper digit one step. tools/sim_carry.py computes the pinion's path.
  • Printing a stack: print it with the biggest gear on the bed (flip marks the ones that print upside down). Keep a stack's gears on neighboring layers. Across a skipped layer, the smaller gear's teeth hang 2.5 mm out over nothing.
  • Gears stay 0.5 mm clear of the walls. Corner blocks (4 mm square) carry 2.5 mm pins into the lid.
  • A port's notch runs from the wall top down to just under its layer. Its width is the gear's chord at the inner face of the wall, plus 0.5 mm each side.

Colors

Parts are colored by role, not by box, so a signal can be followed across a layout without reading a lid: energy comes in at red, enters a box at blue, leaves at orange, and is read at green. Bambu PLA Basic names, one filament per role:

Role Parts Color
Structure shell, keepers, sleeves, caps bone (warm off-white) #EDE3D0
Drive crank wheel and knob, a winder, a settable knob red #D63A2F
Input W and S port gears, a selector's lever blue #1F6FD6
Output E and N port gears orange #F28C28
Display dials, digit wheels, window lips, a follower's tip, a flag green #2FA84F
Transmission inner gears, pins white #EDE8DA

Each lid carries the box's name and, beside it, its function as a symbol (x¼, +, ∫, →, ↺). The export writes one 3MF per part, with an object per color. The pocket tools keep their slate and yellow; the two series are meant to look different.

Families

Every box belongs to one family, named in docs/boxes/INDEX.md. The family colors the lid; shells, keepers, sleeves and caps stay bone, so the role colors (red drive, blue input, orange output, green display, white inner gears) read the same on every box. Family colors never use the role hues blue, orange, green or white; Drives keep red.

Lettering

Lid text, arrows and symbols are raised 0.6 mm, Tahoma Bold, at least 2.0 mm, in white (the inner-gear white) or black #1B1B1B. Use white when the lid's relative luminance is below 0.18 and black above it; contrast must be at least 3:1. Between 0.15 and 0.25 both pass: use white on saturated colors (red, magenta). Dial and digit marks stay black on green. A part on a lid in the lid's own color (the crank wheel, SPRING's knob and plunger, PUSH's key on a red lid) prints white instead (gbs_on_lid()).

Checks

tools/check_fit.sh checks every box in the catalog, then the demo layout:

  • Nothing that turns touches anything, at five input angles across one tooth.
  • The fixed parts only rest on each other.
  • Every stack drops in from above in order (ports first).
  • Every stack is captured.
  • A turned box can't sit beside another.
  • Neighbors in the demo touch nowhere, including their meshed port gears.
