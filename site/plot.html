PLOT ∿

A plotter that never runs out of paper. A tread carries a green spot across the lid for time, and a peg in a channel carries the whole tread up and down for the signal. Drive both and the spot draws your input against time, like the spot on an oscilloscope.

2x1 cells · W in: time (16) · S in: signal (16, west cell) · no outputs · cost 7 on W, 3 on S

How it works

Two inputs, two axes

Time: the tread (W)

Your looping tread: 36 printed links on two eight-flat sprockets. The top run crosses the lid west to east, 48 mm per turn of the W port, 96 mm per crank turn. Links 0 and 18 are green with a dot. They sit half a loop apart, so as one spot dives over the east sprocket the other comes up over the west one.

Signal: the yoke (S)

S turns rotor R through one idler, K. A peg 8 mm out on R's disc runs in the east-west slot of a yoke plate. The plate is the bottom of a frame that rides on both sprocket shafts, between the two halves of each sprocket. When the peg moves north, the frame pushes the sprockets along their D-shafts and the whole tread moves north with it.

Drive: miters over T

The sprocket shafts are horizontal, so W reaches them through a pair of 18-tooth miter gears. T's miter sits on its dial shaft above the lid. An arch carries the east shaft over it. Inside, W climbs to layer 3 through A and runs east over the signal gears through X and Y to T: four meshes, so the top run moves east.

Numbers

Why every sweep draws the same wave

R has 18 teeth against the port's 16, so one wave takes 1.125 port turns. At the same port speed the tread moves 54 mm in that time. The markers are 108 mm apart round the 216 mm loop: exactly two waves. Each spot starts its sweep at the same point of the wave, so the trace stands still. On a scope this is called a trigger. Feed W and S from different drives and the trace rolls from one sweep to the next.

The inputs were swapped on 27 Oct (Paul's pick). Time comes in from the west, so a column of BRANCH or BUS boxes beside a stack of plots starts every one. The signal comes up from the box underneath, which is where SQUARE, RAMP and TURN put their output: a wave box sits directly under its plot. A MOTOR west of PLOT, on its row, is an always-forward time base.

Layouts

What to plug in

  • Triggered, one crank: CRANK at (0, 0). Its E output runs through TURN (1, 0) up into S, the signal. Its N output runs through TAP (0, 1) into W, the time. PLOT sits at (1, 1) to (2, 1). Costs 6 and 10, both inside the crank's 20.
  • Free-running sweep: a MOTOR west of PLOT, on its row, runs the time base, and a CRANK directly under PLOT's west cell is the signal. Turn it by hand and watch your own wave go by.
  • Waves from below: SQUARE, RAMP or a TURN directly under PLOT's west cell feeds it. Three plots from one motor stack into a tower of 12 boxes: MOTOR, a column of BUS, BRANCH and TAP, and a wave box under each PLOT (the layout study).
  • Other signals: a TURN after HALF, DOUBLE, SHIFT or REVERSE brings any of them up into S.
By design

A display, not a pen

PLOT shows the signal as it happens. The spot leaves no trace, like a scope without persistence. To draw on a card, use WAVE.

Status

Checked, and not yet

Checked in OpenSCAD (and the full fit check on main)

  • After the inputs swap: nothing inside touches at five (signal, time) pairs, and every stack drops in from above in order and is held.
  • Nothing that moves touches anything: at rest, with the yoke at its north end, and at its south end.
  • The fixed parts only rest on each other.
  • The miters mesh cleanly across a whole tooth.
  • Two faults found and fixed: a sprocket half with its D-flat on the wrong side, and link plates sitting inside the sprocket flats.

Not verified

  • Chordal action: eight flats make the tread speed ripple about ±4 % and lift it 0.6 mm once per link.
  • Print-in-place hinges (0.35 mm a side) and the filament pin that closes the loop.
  • The frame sliding on both shafts. The peg pushes off center, so it may rack.
  • The frame's print needs supports under the arm, or a split arm.
