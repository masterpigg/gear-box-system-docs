CAM ⌒

Turns rotation into up-and-down. A swappable cam disc under the lid lifts a green follower rod. Change the cam and you change the motion.

How it works

W drives the 32-tooth stack C at half the line's speed. A face cam presses onto C's D-key.

The cam's top face rises and falls around the turn. A follower rod rides on it and comes up through a bush in the lid, from 0 to 8 mm.

Three cams come with it: sine (an eccentric, smooth up and down), snail (a steady rise, then a sudden drop) and heart (steady up, steady down).

Lift the lid to swap cams. The cam's peak sits just under the lid, which holds C down.

Why it is built this way

  • A face cam, not an edge cam. The follower moves vertically, so it can poke through the lid where you can see it, without any side space.
  • Swappable. The shape of the motion is just the shape of the disc, which makes the cam a function you can change by hand.

How to assemble it

  1. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin. The pin is the bigger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it.
  2. Build it in this order: port W, then C, then a cam disc on C's D-key, then the keeper, then the lid with the follower in its bush.
  3. Put a keeper on each side that has ports: its legs go in the notches and its flange in the recess on the wall top.
  4. Put the lid on. Its corner holes take the pins on the corner blocks.

To join it to a neighbor, lower it onto the neighbor's tongues from above.

What it teaches

  • Rotary to linear motion.
  • A cam profile is a graph: height as a function of angle.
  • Where it's used: engine valves, sewing machines, automata.
