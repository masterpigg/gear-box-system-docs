ADDER +

The gear system's first box with two inputs that both count. Turn W one turn and E turns half a turn; turn S one turn and E turns half a turn; turn both and E turns a whole turn. Turn them opposite ways and E stays still: subtraction comes free. A dial on the lid turns with the result.

How it works

A planetary set has three parts that turn round one center: the sun, the ring, and the carrier that holds the planets. Lock any one and the other two are a plain gear pair.

Let all three turn and the carrier's speed is a weighted sum of the other two: (12 × sun + 36 × ring) / 48.

W reaches the sun through two meshes and S reaches the ring through two, so both turn the same way for forward. The gearing makes the two weights equal: the sun is driven at 1/8 turn per tooth of travel and the ring at 1/24, and 12/8 = 36/24.

The carrier then turns (W + S) / 32 in pitch-line terms, and the output train (48 → 12 → 24/16 → 16) turns that into (W + S) / 2 at E.

Why it is built this way

  • An average, not a sum. A full sum would need X's lower gear bigger than its upper one, and then X couldn't go in past the ring. A ×2 box after the adder gives the sum, and makes a good lesson about scaling.
  • One cell wide, two tall. The ring is 50 mm across, so it needs the middle of a two-cell box. The output train uses the upper cell.
  • The ring floats. It turns on three posts underneath, the carrier above holds it down, and the three planets keep it centered.
  • The inside teeth are turned 5° against the outside ones so the planets mesh at the start. The fit check found this value.

How to assemble it

  1. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin. The pin is the bigger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it.
  2. Build it in this order: ports W, S and E; then J, I and the sun stack P on the center pin; then the ring on its three posts round the sun; then the planets on the carrier's pins, carrier turned over and lowered onto the center pin (turn it a little until the planets mesh); then X, Y and its sleeve; then the keepers, the lid, and the dial pressed onto the carrier's D-key. Print the planet three times.
  3. Put a keeper on each side that has ports: its legs go in the notches and its flange in the recess on the wall top.
  4. Put the lid on. Its corner holes take the pins on the corner blocks.

To join it to a neighbor, lower it onto the neighbor's tongues from above.

What it teaches

  • A differential adds: the same mechanism that lets a car's wheels turn at different speeds on a bend.
  • Mechanical computers, from tide predictors to Second World War gun directors, added with differentials.
  • Subtraction is the same box with one input turned backwards (a REVERSE box in front of S).
