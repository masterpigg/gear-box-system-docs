BRANCH

Routing · fit-checked, not printed · 1x1 cells · cost 3 (E), 3 (on north)

The power line comes up from the south, leaves east into the row, and goes on north. It is TAP with a north port, or TEE turned onto the power line: the box that lets one drive start a row in every cell of a column.

What it does

S in; E and N out, both x1, forward. A column of BRANCHes is a power line with a tap in every cell. Until now a column could only start a row and stop (TAP), or start two rows from a two-cell box (BUS).

Train: S (16, layer 2) → I (16, layer 2) → C (24 on layer 2, 24 on layer 1, dial) → E (16, layer 1) and N (16, layer 2). Three meshes to each.

Torque cost

3 stages to E and 3 on north. In the sandbox it starts a row the way TAP does, and adds 3 to the column.

How it works

TAP is TEE mirrored on the diagonal: TEE's C sits where it meets both E and N, so TAP's C, the mirror, sits where it meets both E and N too. TAP leaves its N port out; BRANCH puts it in. The layer-2 gear of C, which TAP uses only to take the line from I, also drives N.

Why it was missing

Three plots from one motor (Paul, 2026-10-27) needed a column that starts a row in each cell. A search over every placement in a 6x6 grid, with the sandbox's own rules (lanes, the power line, the motor's budget of 15), found no layout with today's boxes that stacks three plots one above the other; side by side they take 26 boxes. With BRANCH, and PLOT taking its time on W and its signal from below, they stack in a tower of 12 (layout study: threeplots.html). Without the PLOT change, BRANCH alone is not enough for the tower.

Parts and colors

As TAP, plus one port gear: shell, keepers (S, E, N) and the band bone; lid Sunflower Yellow with black lettering; S blue; E and N orange; I and C white; dial green.
