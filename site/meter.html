METER ◔

An instrument panel for a column. Two rows end in it, each on its own dial, and the power line runs straight through with a third dial, so you can watch three motions side by side.

1×2 cells · in: W1, W2, S · out: N · cost 2 (5 on the line)

Exploded view

What it does

W1, W2, S→three dials→N

Two cells tall: a dial for the row arriving on W in each cell, and the power line passed from S to N with its own dial.

2 stages on each row, 5 stages on the line from S to N.

How it works

Each west port meshes a 16-tooth dial stack right beside it, out toward the end wall. The rows end here: the dials show what arrived.

The N/S line is a chain of four equal idlers up the middle of the box, on layer 2, so it never meets the rows on layer 1. The third one, C, carries the line's dial.

The row dials sit out by the S and N walls and the line's dial in the middle, so the three never collide on the lid.

Why it is built this way

  • On the power line. The old METER read three lanes on one side, and only the old BUS could feed it. Now the rows that end in it can come from anywhere.
  • The line passes through. A meter that ended the column would be one more end box; passing it on lets the meter sit anywhere in a column.
  • The rows end here. Passing the rows on to E as well doesn't fit: each row would have to cross the N/S chain, and a dial's hub runs up through every layer. A tee or tap before the meter keeps a row going if you need it.
  • A straight chain. Four 24-tooth idlers fill the 112 mm between S and N exactly, so no stack prints upside down. The cost is five meshes; a 3-mesh train would need two 48-tooth gears, which hit the W ports' pins.

How to assemble it

  1. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin. The pin is the bigger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it.
  2. Build it in this order: the four ports, then D1 and D2, then A, B, C and D, then the keepers and lid, then the dials.
  3. Put a keeper on each side that has ports: its legs go in the notches and its flange in the recess on the wall top.
  4. Put the lid on. Its corner holes take the pins on the corner blocks.

To join it to a neighbor, lower it onto the neighbor's tongues from above.

What it teaches

  • Reading a signal, and reading several at once to compare them (×1 against ×¼, say).
  • Layers keep channels apart: the rows on layer 1, the line on layer 2.
  • Backlash: each row dial is one mesh from its port, so it reads the row as cleanly as possible.
