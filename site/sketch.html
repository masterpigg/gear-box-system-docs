SKETCH ✎

An etch-a-sketch on a gear box lid. WAVE's pen stands where two sliding bars cross over a card. Turn the two red knobs to draw. Or drive the two south ports, and the box becomes a plotter that draws a clock's wave against time, like an oscilloscope on paper.

2×2 cells · SX in (x, time) + SY in (y, signal), both on the south · cost 5 each · no outputs

The idea

Two bars, one pen, two ways to drive it

A card lies on the lid. A white carriage slides east and west on a rail along the south edge. A green pen bar, one level higher, slides north and south on a rail along the east edge. Both bars are slotted, and the pen drops through both slots where they cross. Move a bar and the pen moves with it.

Each bar has a rack at one end, and each rack is turned by a red knob with a pinion under it: that's the toy. The same pinions are also the top of a gear train down to an input port on the south side. Drive the ports from other boxes and the box draws by itself.

Try it

Draw with the knobs, or let a clock draw

Drag a knob around (or focus it and use the arrow keys). Then switch to plot, pick a shaper and press Crank: the time base moves the pen across while the shaper moves it up and down, at the model's real ratios.

Each crank turn of the time base is 25.1 mm across, and the shaper's clock makes one cycle a crank turn, so every wave repeats every 25.1 mm. Because time and signal come from one shaft, crank back and crank again and the pen retraces the same line: that's triggering.

How it's built

Two trains that never cross

Both trains have four meshes, an even count, so a forward port turns the pinion clockwise, and a clockwise pinion moves both racks the right way: east and north. The x train lives in the south-west quarter, the y train in the south-east and east. The middle, where DECK's lock boss sits under a 2×2's inner node, stays empty.

  • The racks cross one level apart at the south-east. The y rail stops short of the carriage's path for that reason.
  • End of travel: each rack has 19 teeth. About 30 mm out it runs off its pinion, so cranking on can't break anything. Turn back and it catches again (WAVE does the same).
  • Backlash: about 0.2 mm through a knob, about 1.4 mm on a y reversal through SY (the step-up doubles it). A square wave's edges lean that much.
  • Colors: bone shell with a charcoal band, charcoal lid with white lettering (Readouts), blue ports, white inner gears, red knobs (a drive you set), white carriage, green pen bar and pen (display). Each plate stays within one AMS.
Your question

One motor, three plots: square, triangle, sine

The trick is to stop running wires to each plotter and give every plotter the same four-box column. A TAP takes the power line into the row, a TEE splits it into time (north, into SX) and signal (east, into the shaper), and the shaper sits right under SKETCH's east cell, so its N output runs SY. Nothing goes round the side. The power row underneath carries one motor along with plain WIREs (each pair can be one long WIRE 2).

row 1-2:   .    [ SKETCH ]   .    [ SKETCH ]   .    [ SKETCH ]
row 0:    TAP   TEE  SQUARE  TAP   TEE   RAMP  TAP   TEE  RAMP sine
row -1: [MOTOR]  WIRE WIRE  TEE  WIRE WIRE  TURN

The torque budget fits

A TAP restarts the budget. Each plotter's longest path is TAP 3 + TEE 3 + shaper 4 + SKETCH 5 = 15, exactly the motor's 15. The power row is 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14. Without the TAPs the third plot would be about 30 stages from the motor.

Comparing gear setups

Put the setup you want to compare between the TEE and the shaper. That takes a cell, so change the shaper's plates instead, or change the time base. A ÷2 between TAP and TEE slows both time and signal, and the picture doesn't change at all. (Why not?)

Also new

A sine plate for RAMP

The long wires you asked for (WIRE 2 and 3, POLE 2 and 3) are on main too, so this one uses those instead of making its own. RAMP gets a new RS plate pair: the plate's radius rises and falls as a cosine, so the follower draws a sine.
