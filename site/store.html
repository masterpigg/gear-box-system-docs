The arbor winds the spring; the spring turns the barrel (40 teeth, layer 1); the barrel drives E (16). One mesh, so E turns forward as the barrel runs clockwise, at 2.5 times its speed. Wind up to one turn (24 clicks) for up to 2.5 turns of E.

Torque cost

Drive budget: 0 stages

A drive, like CRANK: about 2 stages of budget. A full wind gives about 28 N·mm at the barrel and 11 N·mm at E, falling off evenly as it runs down, so it drives one or two boxes rather than three.

How it works

The mainspring is a printed PETG spiral, 1 mm thick and 7 mm tall, four coils from 6 to 15 mm out. Its inner end hooks into a slot in the arbor, its outer end into a groove in the barrel's wall.

Winding: the knob turns the arbor clockwise and the coils wrap tighter round it. A flexure pawl, printed with the shell, clicks over the arbor's ratchet and holds each click.

Holding: a plunger through the lid sits in one of 12 notches in the barrel's rim, so the barrel can't turn.

Running: lift the plunger and the spring turns the barrel clockwise, and the barrel's 40 teeth turn E. Let go and the plunger rides the rim and drops into the next notch.

Why it is built this way

  • A barrel, as in a clock. The spring sits between two parts that turn: you wind the inside, and the outside drives the train. Winding never moves the line, and running never moves the knob.
  • One turn of wind. A 266 mm band packs onto the arbor at about 1.2 turns. One turn bends it 1.2 % at the surface, well inside what PETG takes back.
  • Wound from the lid, not from W. A W port on layer 1 would sit where the barrel's teeth are. A knob on the arbor needs no extra gears, and the box is a drive like CRANK and MOTOR.
  • The pawl's arm is long (22 mm), so its post stands clear of the barrel's teeth.

How to assemble it

  1. Wipe a PTFE dry film on every pin. The pin is the bigger loss in a stage, and the lube roughly halves it.
  2. Build it in this order: port E, then the barrel on the center pin, cup up; the mainspring in the barrel with its outer hook in the wall's groove; push the pawl's arm back and lower the arbor through the spring, turning it until the inner hook drops into its slot; keeper on E and the lid on, knob pressed onto the arbor's D-key; drop the plunger through its hole and turn the barrel by its gear until it falls into a notch. Print the mainspring in PETG, flat. Nothing is bought.
  3. Put a keeper on each side that has ports: its legs go in the notches and its flange in the recess on the wall top.
  4. Put the lid on. Its corner holes take the pins on the corner blocks.

To join it to a neighbor, lower it onto the neighbor's tongues from above.

What it teaches

  • Springs store energy. A clock spring stores it as bending, and gives nearly all of it back.
  • A barrel separates winding from running. Every clockwork toy and mechanical watch has one.
  • A ratchet and a stop make a battery and its switch, all from plastic.
